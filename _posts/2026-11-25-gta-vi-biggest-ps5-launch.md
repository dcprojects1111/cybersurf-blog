The tech world keeps moving, and a few new bits of news this week could make life a little easier or more fun for everyday Australians. From smarter watches to a game that’s breaking records, here’s what you need to know without the jargon.

## Apple Watch teams up with CarPlay for hands‑free navigation

Apple has added a new feature that lets your Apple Watch work directly with Apple CarPlay, the system that mirrors your iPhone’s maps and music onto a car’s infotainment screen. When you start a drive, the watch can give you gentle taps (called haptic feedback) to guide you through turns, keeping your eyes on the road.

**What this means for you**  
- If you already own an Apple Watch and a CarPlay‑enabled car, the feature is ready to use – just enable it in the Watch app.  
- It can be a handy way to stay aware of directions without glancing at the phone or dashboard.  
- No extra hardware is needed, but you’ll need a compatible iPhone and a CarPlay‑ready vehicle, which most newer models sold in Australia have.

## Apple Watch can tell the time without you looking

A new trick on the Apple Watch lets it share the time through subtle vibrations, even using Morse code (the series of short and long taps used in old telegraph messages). The watch offers three modes: a simple tap for the hour, a double tap for hour + minute, and a Morse‑code option for those who like a bit of novelty.

**What this means for you**  
- You can check the time discreetly during meetings, at the gym, or while cooking, without raising your wrist.  
- It’s easy to set up in the Watch app under “Accessibility → Haptic Time.”  
- No extra cost is involved; it works on any recent Apple Watch model already in Australian hands.

## GTA VI becomes the biggest PlayStation launch ever

Rockstar’s new Grand Theft Auto VI has smashed sales records, pulling in $3.79 billion on its first day and attracting 39 million unique players on the PS5. It’s the biggest launch in PlayStation history across all five console generations.

**What this means for you**  
- If you own a PlayStation 5, the game is already available in Australian stores and online, so you can jump in whenever you like.  
- The huge player base means plenty of online activity, but also a busier network – you might experience occasional lag during peak times.  
- No special hardware is required beyond the PS5 you already have; just make sure your console’s software is up to date.

## Change a Roku TV setting to watch movies the way filmmakers intended

Most Roku TVs default to a 60 fps (frames per second) refresh rate, which can make fast‑moving movies look a bit different from how they were shot. By adjusting a simple setting, you can switch to the original refresh rate the film was produced in, often 24 fps, giving a smoother, more cinematic feel.

**What this means for you**  
- If you have a Roku TV at home, go into Settings → Display → Refresh Rate and select the option that matches the movie’s native frame rate.  
- The change is reversible, so you can switch back for sports or video games that benefit from higher frame rates.  
- This tweak works on all Roku TV models sold in Australia, and there’s no extra cost involved.

## Quick takeaway

- Apple Watch now offers hands‑free CarPlay navigation and a discreet way to check the time via haptics.  
- GTA VI’s record‑breaking launch means the game is widely available and bustling with players on PS5.  
- A simple Roku TV setting can give you a more authentic movie‑watching experience.
