Three of the biggest names in Australian shopping and everyday tech have had trouble this week. Coles has reported a cyber incident, Woolworths customers say their digital gift cards were emptied, and Apple had a large outage. They are not connected, as far as anyone has said so far. But each one can touch your day, whether you are a shopper, a parent, a tradie or a business owner. Here is what has been confirmed, what has not, and what it could mean for you.

## Who is affected, at a glance

- **Everyday shoppers:** anyone who shops at Coles or Woolworths, holds a digital gift card, or pays with Apple Pay.
- **Families and students:** anyone who keeps photos, school work or contacts in iCloud, or signs in to apps with an Apple Account.
- **Small business owners and sole traders:** anyone who takes Apple Pay at the counter, buys gift cards for staff or customers, relies on iCloud or Apple devices to run the business, or supplies Coles.
- **Everyone:** these events are followed by a wave of scam messages that use the big names to look believable.

## Coles: a cyber incident, with few details

On Monday 16 November, Coles told the stock exchange it was investigating a cyber security incident. Coles says it has not found evidence that customer, team or supplier data has been accessed or stolen. Shopping in stores and online is carrying on as normal. Coles says gift cards are working, it sees no sign of impact on Flybuys accounts, and it has not seen problems with its Coles Plus or delivery subscriptions. It is also looking into a small number of Liquorland click-and-collect problems, and it has not said whether those are related.

What we do not know: how the incident happened, which systems were involved, when it was detected, or who is behind it. Coles calls it an "incident", not an attack, and says it will update customers as appropriate. It asks customers to watch for suspicious links and requests for personal details, and to use a different password for each service.

**How it could affect you**
- **If you shop at Coles or use Flybuys:** Coles says it has found nothing so far, so there is nothing you must do today. But early findings can change. If it later turns out that customer details were taken, expect fake emails and texts that use the Coles name. Be wary of anything saying your Flybuys or account is "locked" or needs "verifying".
- **If you supply Coles or work with them:** Coles has asked suppliers to be cautious. Expect scammers to send fake invoices or "updated bank details" emails that look like they come from Coles. Check any change to payment details by phoning a number you already have.
- **If you use the same password elsewhere:** if a password for any of your accounts is ever exposed, criminals try it on other sites. A different password for each service means one leak does not become ten.

## Woolworths: gift cards drained

Separately, an ABC investigation found dozens of Woolworths customers saying their digital WISH gift cards were emptied by purchases they did not make. Losses of up to $500 were reported. One Brisbane customer said a new $500 card was spent at a Big W in Victoria, and a Sydney customer said a $400 card bought on 9 November was drained. Many of the cards were bought through the Everyday Rewards site or app, some during a 4 per cent discount promotion.

Woolworths has acknowledged an issue with a number of e-gift cards, apologised, and says it is treating the reports as a priority. Replacement cards are one of the options for affected customers, who can contact Gift Card Support.

What we do not know: how the card details were obtained, whether Woolworths itself was breached, or how many people are affected. The reports we found say there is no evidence linking this to the Coles incident, and that the two are being looked at separately.

**How it could affect you**
- **If you hold a digital gift card:** the money can be gone if the card details are taken before you spend it. Do not leave balances sitting for weeks. Spend them soon, and check your balance now.
- **If you buy gift cards as presents or staff rewards:** a lost card is hard to get back. Check the card the day you give it, and keep the receipt and purchase email.
- **If you run a business:** digital gift cards are a common staff and customer reward. If you buy them in bulk, spend them quickly or keep the details somewhere secure.
- **If something goes wrong:** report it to the retailer straight away, keep your receipt, and tell your bank if you paid by card. You can report scams at Scamwatch.

## Apple: a large outage, blamed on an internal update

Early on Wednesday morning Australian time (Tuesday in the US), Apple services went down. iCloud sign-in, the App Store and Apple Music were affected, and people also reported problems with Apple Pay, Apple Card and Apple Account logins. Some users said they could not set up a new iPhone. Some people were signed out of iCloud and then hit errors when they tried to sign back in.

Apple said an internal update caused the outages, not a cyberattack. By the US evening, which was Wednesday morning here, its status page showed services back to normal. Apple has not explained exactly what went wrong, and the reports we found do not say how Australians were affected.

**How it could affect you**
- **If you pay with Apple Pay:** when Apple Pay has trouble, your card in the phone may not work at the till. Keep a physical card or some cash as a backup.
- **If you rely on iCloud:** your photos, backups, contacts and email can stop syncing or become unreachable for a few hours. Keep an important copy somewhere else too, such as a computer, an external drive or a second cloud service.
- **If your Apple Account is your key to everything:** many people sign in to other apps and sites with Apple, and use it to approve logins. When Apple's sign-in is down, you can be locked out of those too. Write down your recovery details and know your backup login method.
- **If you run a business on Apple devices:** schools and workplaces that manage iPhones and iPads through Apple's business tools can also be affected. Have a plan for what staff do when the devices cannot sign in.
- **After any outage, watch for scams:** fake "your Apple Account is locked" emails and texts spike. Do not click them. Open Settings on your phone, or go to apple.com directly.

## What to do now

1. **Use a different password for every account.** A password manager makes this easy.
2. **Turn on two-step sign-in** for email, banking, Apple and your supermarket accounts.
3. **Spend gift cards soon,** and check any balance you hold today.
4. **Keep a backup way to pay and a backup copy of what matters.**
5. **Be careful with unexpected messages** that mention Coles, Woolworths, Apple or your accounts. Go to the company's own app or website instead of clicking.
6. **Report scams** to Scamwatch or ReportCyber, and tell your bank straight away if money has gone.
