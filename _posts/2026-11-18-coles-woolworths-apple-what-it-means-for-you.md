Coles is investigating a cyber incident. Woolworths customers say their digital gift cards were emptied. Apple's online services went down. Nobody has linked the three, and the reports we read say there is no evidence they are connected. But each one touches ordinary Australian life, so this is how it could affect you, whether you shop, study, run a business or just own an iPhone.

Here is what is confirmed, what is not, and what is sensible to do. There is no need to panic.

## Who is affected

- **Shoppers:** anyone who buys from Coles or Woolworths, uses Flybuys or Everyday Rewards, or holds a digital gift card.
- **Parents and students:** anyone using iCloud, Apple sign-in, or school tools that run on Apple services.
- **Small business owners and sole traders:** anyone who buys gift cards for staff or customers, relies on Apple devices, or deals with Coles.
- **Suppliers:** businesses that sell to Coles.
- **Every iPhone user:** an Apple sign-in problem can lock you out of more than one app.
- **Everyone:** big news like this is often used by scammers as bait. That is a general pattern, not something these reports confirm.

## Coles: an incident, few details

On Monday morning, 16 November, Coles told the Australian Securities Exchange it was investigating a cyber security incident. Coles says it has not found evidence that customer, team or supplier data has been compromised. These are early findings from a continuing investigation.

Coles says shopping in stores and online is running as normal. Gift cards are working, it sees no sign of impact on Flybuys accounts, and it has found no issues with Coles Plus. A small number of Liquorland click-and-collect customers are having problems, which Coles is looking into.

**Still unknown:** when it was detected, which systems were involved, how it happened, and who is behind it. Coles says it is working with the Australian Cyber Security Centre and international specialists, and will update customers through its corporate website.

**What it means for you**
- **Shoppers:** Coles says no action is needed right now, but asks you to watch for suspicious links and unexpected requests for personal details. It also recommends strong passwords that you do not reuse across services.
- **Suppliers:** Coles has asked suppliers to take similar care. If anyone emails about changed bank details, phone a number you already hold before paying.

## Woolworths: drained WISH gift cards

An ABC investigation, as reported by The Cyber Express and Australian Cyber Security Magazine, found dozens of customers whose digital WISH gift cards were emptied by purchases they did not recognise. Some lost up to $500. A Brisbane customer lost a full $500 on a new card, spent at a Big W in Victoria. A Sydney customer bought a $400 card on 9 November, and it was spent at a Big W in another suburb before he could use it. Most of the cards were bought through the Everyday Rewards website or app, including during a 4 per cent discount promotion. Customers said they had not shared their card details.

Woolworths has acknowledged an issue affecting a number of e-gift cards, apologised, and says it is treating the reports as a priority. Affected customers can contact Gift Card Support or register through the Everyday Gifting website, and replacement cards may be issued where warranted.

**Still unknown:** how the card details were obtained, whether Woolworths' systems were breached, and how many people are affected.

**What it means for you**
- **Gift card holders:** digital cards can be spent with electronic details alone, so an unused balance carries some risk. Check yours and consider using it sooner.
- **Gift buyers and businesses:** if you buy digital cards as presents or staff rewards, keep the purchase email and receipt. If you notice an unrecognised transaction, contact Gift Card Support.

## Apple: a big outage, blamed on an update

On Tuesday in the US, which was Wednesday in Australia, Apple's iCloud sign-in, App Store and Apple Music were hit by outages. The status page said iCloud sign-in affected "all users". Apple School Manager, iCloud Mail, iCloud.com, Schoolwork and the Volume Purchase Program were added later. Some users reported being unable to activate a new iPhone, and sign-in problems could cause repeated password prompts. A Fox News live blog also said reports of problems with Apple Pay, Apple Music and Apple Card spiked on the day.

Apple told TechCrunch an internal update caused the outages, not a cyberattack. By 6pm Tuesday US Eastern time, its status page showed everything resolved. The reports we could open do not say how Australians were affected.

**What it means for you**
- **iPhone users:** if Apple sign-in fails, other logins that rely on it could fail too. Know your backup sign-in method.
- **Apple Pay users:** if Apple Pay has trouble at the till, a physical card or some cash is a handy backup.
- **Parents, students and schools:** tools such as Schoolwork and Apple School Manager were on the affected list.
- **Business owners:** have a plan for when staff cannot sign in, and keep important files in more than one place.
- **After any outage:** be wary of messages saying your Apple Account is locked. Open Settings on your phone, or type apple.com yourself.

## What to do now

1. **Use a different password for each account.** A password manager helps.
2. **Turn on two-step sign-in** for email, banking, Apple and supermarket accounts.
3. **Check any gift card balance** you hold.
4. **Do not click unexpected messages** mentioning Coles, Woolworths or Apple. Use the official app or website.
5. **Report scams** to Scamwatch or ReportCyber, and tell your bank at once if money has gone.
