The tech world is buzzing with a few big sales, a new phone launch in Asia, a whisper of a fresh fold‑able design, and a rare lifetime cloud‑storage offer. Here’s what you need to know without the jargon.

## Galaxy S26 series and Pixel 11 hit big Black Friday discounts

Amazon has started its early Black Friday sale ahead of the official UK date of 27 November. The retailer is offering steep price cuts on Samsung’s flagship Galaxy S26 series – up to £570 off – and on Google’s Pixel 11 series, plus lower prices for Oppo’s Find X10. Samsung’s own promotion claims discounts of up to £300 on its Galaxy range.

**What this means for you**  
- If you were already planning to upgrade your phone, the sale could shave a few hundred pounds off the bill.  
- The deals are listed in British pounds; Australian pricing has not been confirmed, so you’ll need to check with local retailers or your mobile provider.  
- Early‑bird sales can run out quickly, so act fast if a particular colour or storage size is important to you.  

*Tip:* When buying online, make sure the site uses HTTPS (the little padlock in the address bar) and double‑check that the seller is reputable to avoid phishing scams.

## Samsung Galaxy A18 4G arrives in Singapore with a different chip

Samsung’s budget‑friendly Galaxy A18 4G, first shown in Europe in September, is now on sale in Singapore. The key difference is the processor under the hood: the European model uses Samsung’s own Exynos 1610 chip, while the Singapore version is powered by MediaTek’s Helio G99+ chip. Apart from the processor swap, the two phones share the same design, camera setup and battery capacity.

**What this means for you**  
- The change in chip is unlikely to affect everyday tasks such as calling, texting, browsing or using social media.  
- If you travel between Australia and Singapore and plan to use the same device, the performance should feel familiar.  
- Prices and availability in Australia have not been announced, so keep an eye on local Samsung announcements or your favourite retailer.  

*Tip:* When buying a phone that’s been released in another market, verify that the device supports the cellular bands used by Australian carriers.

## Rumour: Huawei may launch a “wide‑form” nova Flip next year

A tipster on China’s Weibo platform has hinted that Huawei’s next nova Flip could move away from the small clamshell design and adopt a wider, more tablet‑like form factor. The rumored device would feature a 6‑inch display that folds outward, offering a larger screen while still fitting in a pocket.

**What this means for you**  
- At this stage it’s only a rumor, so there’s no confirmed price, launch date or specifications.  
- If you’re interested in a foldable phone but find current models too tiny, a wider version could be appealing once it’s officially announced.  
- Until Huawei confirms the design, it’s safest to stick with the devices you already own or consider other proven options.  

*Tip:* Be cautious of pre‑order scams that target hype around unconfirmed products. Only purchase from authorised retailers once the phone is officially launched.

## pCloud offers a lifetime cloud‑storage deal for Black Friday

Cloud‑storage services usually charge a monthly or yearly subscription. pCloud, a company that lets you keep photos, documents and videos online, is running a Black Friday “lifetime” deal – you pay once and keep the storage forever. The promotion is marketed as a way to cut down on the endless stream of subscription fees many of us juggle.

**What this means for you**  
- If you’re already paying for multiple cloud services (iPhone iCloud, a password manager, streaming apps, etc.), a one‑off purchase could simplify your bills.  
- The offer is global, but the price is listed in US dollars; Australian pricing has not been confirmed, so check the pCloud website for local currency information.  
- Lifetime plans are rare, so make sure you read the fine print about data security and backup options before committing.  

*Tip:* Use a strong, unique password for your cloud account and enable two‑factor authentication (a second code sent to your phone) to protect your files from unauthorized access.
