The tech world keeps moving, and even if you don’t spend hours online it can still affect the gadgets you already have at home. Today’s updates include a smoother way to turn speech into text, cheaper cameras that can recognise faces, a new way to spice up WhatsApp chats, a quirky desktop‑like mode for the iPhone Duo and a rumour about Apple testing a desktop operating system on iPads.

## Better voice‑to‑text on iPhone, Mac and other devices  
Wispr Flow, an AI‑powered dictation app, is rolling out a new “Canto‑2” speech‑to‑text model. The update promises fewer mistakes when it transcribes what you say, and it is better at recognising names and technical words.

**What this means for you**  
- If you already use Wispr Flow on your phone or computer, you should see more accurate notes, emails or messages without having to edit as much.  
- The upgrade is automatic, so just make sure your app is set to update in the App Store or Mac App Store.  
- The app works on iPhone, iPad, Mac and other platforms, so you can use it wherever you type.  

## Google’s “Familiar Faces” AI now works on cheap Onn cameras  
A recent Google Home update adds its “Familiar Faces” feature – which can identify people in the camera view – to Walmart’s budget‑friendly Onn Outdoor Camera ($35) and Onn Floodlight Camera ($77). The feature still requires a Google Home Premium subscription. The update also adds a remote control in the Google Home app to trigger a 30‑second security siren while you watch live video.

**What this means for you**  
- You can get face‑recognition on a camera that costs less than a typical smart‑home device, but you’ll need to pay for the Google Home Premium subscription to use the feature.  
- If you already have an Onn camera, check the Google Home app for the update and follow the prompts to enable Familiar Faces.  
- For Australians, keep an eye on local retailers such as JB Hi‑Fi, Harvey Norman or Amazon Australia for the cameras and verify the subscription cost in Australian dollars before signing up.  

## WhatsApp now lets you send stickers with sound  
WhatsApp has started rolling out “sound stickers” in version 2.26.47.1, initially to some beta testers on the Google Play Store. You can turn a short video from your gallery into an animated sticker and then add a sound track – either the original audio, a preset effect, or a music file you import.

**What this means for you**  
- It’s a free way to add a bit of personality to chats without needing a separate app.  
- Creating a sound sticker is as simple as tapping “Create sticker,” picking a video, and then hitting the new audio button.  
- Because the stickers are sent over the internet, they will use a small amount of mobile data; if you’re on a limited plan, you may want to create them while connected to Wi‑Fi.  

## Screens app gives iPhone Duo a tiny “MacBook” mode  
The Screens app, which already added two desktop‑style features earlier this month, now offers a “tiny laptop mode” for iPhone Duo users. The mode turns the phone into a small, MacBook‑like window where you can run multiple apps side‑by‑side, similar to a desktop experience.

**What this means for you**  
- If you own an iPhone Duo, you can try the new mode without buying any extra hardware – just open the Screens app and enable “tiny laptop.”  
- This could be handy for checking emails, browsing the web or working on documents while you’re on the couch or in the garden.  
- The feature is still an app‑based solution, so it won’t replace a real laptop, but it may reduce the need to pull out a separate device for quick tasks.  

## Apple is testing macOS on iPads – but only internally for now  
A report from a trusted Apple insider says Apple is running macOS – the desktop operating system used on Macs – on some iPad models as part of internal testing. The company has not announced any plans to ship iPads with macOS, and the work is purely experimental at this stage.

**What this means for you**  
- There’s no change to the iPad experience you have today; iPadOS – the tablet‑focused version of Apple’s software – will continue to be the operating system on all iPads you can buy.  
- Keep an eye on future announcements if you’re hoping for a more desktop‑like iPad, but for now there’s nothing to act on.  

## Quick takeaway  
- Wispr Flow’s new speech model makes voice dictation more reliable across Apple devices.  
- Google’s face‑recognition now works on inexpensive Onn cameras, but you’ll need a paid subscription.  
- WhatsApp sound stickers let you add short audio clips to animated stickers for free.  
- Screens gives iPhone Duo a desktop‑style window that can replace a tiny laptop for simple tasks.  
- Apple’s macOS‑on‑iPad test is internal only; iPad users won’t see any changes yet.
