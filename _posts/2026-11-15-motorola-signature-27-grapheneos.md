The tech world keeps moving, and a few new gadgets and accessories have just landed in the headlines. Whether you’re thinking about a fresh phone, a handy iPhone stand, a new pair of headphones, or keeping an eye on future camera upgrades, here’s what’s worth knowing today.

## Motorola Signature 27 gets a boost from GrapheneOS support

Motorola’s flagship Signature 27 is still priced at a premium – the manufacturer lists it at US $1,600 (about €1,900). The phone has now been confirmed to run GrapheneOS, a privacy‑focused operating system that replaces the standard Android software with a version that limits data collection and improves security.

**What this means for you**  
- The high price means most Australians will probably wait for discounts before buying.  
- If you value privacy and are comfortable installing a new OS, the GrapheneOS support could make the Signature 27 a more attractive option.  
- Keep an eye on local retailers such as JB Hi‑Fi, Harvey Norman, or your mobile carrier for any price drops or bundle offers.

## ESR’s MagSafe kickstand charger makes the iPhone Duo easier to use

Apple’s iPhone Duo, with its larger inner screen, has sparked a demand for accessories that let you prop it up for video or video‑calls. ESR has released a MagSafe‑compatible kickstand charger that attaches magnetically to the back of the phone, providing both a stable stand and a wireless charging pad.

**What this means for you**  
- You can watch movies, follow recipes, or join a Zoom call without holding the phone, while it charges at the same time.  
- The MagSafe system is built into newer iPhones, so the accessory snaps on without any extra tools.  
- Availability in Australia hasn’t been announced yet; check Amazon Australia, the Apple Store online, or local electronics shops for stock and pricing.

## Beats 360 versus Beats Studio Pro: which headphones should you pick?

Apple’s Beats brand now offers two over‑ear models: the newer Beats 360 and the slightly older Beats Studio Pro, which is still on sale at a lower price. Both provide wireless sound, but they differ in design and battery life.

**What this means for you**  
- **Beats 360** – newer design, likely a bit lighter, and comes with the latest Bluetooth version for smoother connections.  
- **Beats Studio Pro** – still a solid choice, especially if you find it discounted; it may have a longer battery run‑time.  
- Decide based on what you need: if you want the freshest tech and don’t mind paying a bit more, the 360 may suit you; if you’re happy with a proven model and want to save, the Studio Pro could be the better bargain.  
- Look for these headphones at Australian retailers like JB Hi‑Fi, Harvey Norman, or online stores that ship locally.

## Honour Magic 10 series could sport a 200 MP camera with LOFIC tech – it’s still a rumor

A tipster has suggested that Honour’s upcoming Magic 10 series might include a massive 200‑megapixel main camera using something called LOFIC (Low‑Frequency Illumination Capture) technology, which is meant to improve low‑light performance. The claim also mentions a dual‑layer OLED display, but Honour has not confirmed any details.

**What this means for you**  
- Until Honour officially announces the specs, treat the 200 MP figure as a possibility, not a guarantee.  
- If you’re considering a new phone and love high‑resolution photos, you may want to wait for the official launch before deciding.  
- Keep an eye on Australian phone retailers and Honour’s own website for the final specifications and pricing.

## Quick takeaway

- The Motorola Signature 27 could become a more privacy‑friendly option if you’re willing to wait for discounts.  
- ESR’s MagSafe kickstand charger offers a simple way to prop up the iPhone Duo while it charges.  
- Beats 360 is the newest over‑ear model, but the Studio Pro remains a solid, potentially cheaper alternative.  
- Honour’s Magic 10 camera specs are still unconfirmed; hold off on buying until the company releases official details.
