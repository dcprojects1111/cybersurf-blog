*Developing story, last updated 8:00 AM (Brisbane time). We will update this post if things change.*

## What has happened  

1Password has confirmed that an employee’s corporate GitHub account was compromised on 19 November. The attacker used the account to inject malicious code into 1Password’s backend services and to download a limited set of customer records. Stolen items include email‑linked invitation and recovery tokens, as well as internal source code and possible infrastructure credentials.  

The company says the breach did **not** expose password vaults or the encryption keys that protect them. No evidence of full account take‑overs has been found, and only a small number of users have been individually notified. 1Password has disabled the compromised GitHub account, revoked the stolen token, restored clean service versions and rotated any exposed credentials.  

## Does it affect you?  

- If you use 1Password for personal or business accounts, your vault contents remain encrypted and safe.  
- The list of affected users is not public, and it is not yet clear whether any Australian customers are among them.  
- The stolen invitation and recovery tokens can be abused in phishing attempts that impersonate 1Password.  

## What to do right now  

- **Verify any unexpected 1Password emails** – check the sender address, look for spelling oddities, and be cautious of urgent language.  
- **Do not click links** in unsolicited 1Password messages. Instead, open a browser and go directly to the official 1Password website or launch the app.  
- **Watch for unusual account activity** such as unknown provisioning invitations or recovery requests.  
- **Enable two‑factor authentication (2FA)** on your 1Password account if you have not already done so.  
- **Report suspicious emails** to 1Password via their official support channels.  

*General safe‑practice advice:* Regularly back up important business data, keep software up to date, and educate staff on how to recognise phishing attempts.
