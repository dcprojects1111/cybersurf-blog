The tech world keeps moving, even if you’re just scrolling through a feed or sending a quick message. This week’s headlines bring a few changes that could affect how families use social media, how travellers stay connected, and how you capture ideas on your iPhone or iPad.

## X adds parental controls for teen accounts  

X (the platform formerly known as Twitter) announced that it now offers built‑in parental controls for users aged 13‑17. The new tools sit on top of the existing “protected mode” that already hides sensitive media and blocks targeted ads. Parents or guardians can now:

* See how much time a teen has spent on X over the past week.  
* Set a daily time limit that the app will enforce.  
* Require approval before the teen can follow new accounts, effectively making the account private.  
* Limit the “For You” recommendation feed, which curates posts based on the user’s activity.

What this means for you  
If you have a teenager who enjoys chatting on X, the controls give you a simple way to keep an eye on usage without constantly checking the phone. The feature is available globally, but Australian pricing or special bundles haven’t been announced. To start using it, open the X app, go to Settings → Parental Controls, and follow the prompts. If you’re unsure how to set it up, a quick call to your phone provider or a visit to the X help centre can clear things up.

## WhatsApp is testing a travel eSIM feature  

WhatsApp’s latest Android build (version 2.26.47) contains hidden code that points to a future “travel eSIM” option. An eSIM is a digital SIM – the tiny chip that connects your phone to a network – that can be programmed without needing a physical card. The proposed feature would let you:

* Browse available local data plans while you’re abroad, directly inside WhatsApp.  
* Purchase and install the plan with a few taps, no need to visit a store.  
* Turn the plan on or off from the app, so you only pay for what you use.

What this means for you  
If you travel overseas for work or holidays, the ability to buy a data plan inside WhatsApp could save you the hassle of hunting for a local SIM shop. The feature is still under development and isn’t live yet, so you won’t see it on your phone today. Keep your WhatsApp app updated and watch for a notification when the feature rolls out. When it does, you’ll likely need a compatible Android phone (most newer models support eSIMs). Australian carriers haven’t confirmed pricing, so check with your provider or look for updates on the Google Play Store.

## Readdle’s Documents app adds AI‑powered voice notes  

Readdle, the maker of the popular Documents app for iPhone and iPad, has introduced a new Voice Notes feature. Using on‑device AI, the app can record a conversation, then automatically produce a written transcript, a short summary, and even suggested follow‑up actions. Key points:

* Record directly inside Documents – no need for a separate voice‑recorder app.  
* The AI creates a text version of what was said, useful for quick reference.  
* Summaries give you the gist in a few sentences, and follow‑up notes help you remember next steps.

What this means for you  
If you often take meeting notes, interview recordings, or just want to capture a thought without typing, the new Voice Notes can streamline the process. The feature works on any iPhone or iPad that can run the latest version of Documents, which is available worldwide through the App Store. There’s no extra cost mentioned, and because the processing happens on the device, your recordings stay private. To try it, open Documents, tap the new Voice Notes icon, and follow the on‑screen instructions.

## Quick takeaway  

* X’s parental controls give families a straightforward way to monitor and limit teen usage – set it up in the app’s Settings.  
* WhatsApp’s upcoming travel eSIM could let Australian travellers buy local data plans without leaving the app; stay tuned for the official rollout.  
* Readdle’s Documents now turns spoken words into searchable text and handy summaries, making note‑taking on iPhone and iPad faster and more organized.
