## What has happened  
1Password, a popular password‑manager service, has disclosed that a data breach occurred. The breach exposed some customer information as well as internal source‑code files. The company has confirmed the incident but has not released further details about the extent of the data or the exact cause of the breach.

## Does it affect you?  
At this stage it is not yet clear whether any Australian users or Australian‑based businesses were among those whose data was accessed. If you or your team use 1Password for storing passwords, logins or other sensitive information, you should assume there is a possibility that your account could be involved until more information is made public.

## What to do right now  
*General advice for anyone using 1Password or any password‑manager:*  

- **Check your account status** – Log into your 1Password vault and look for any unexpected activity or alerts from the provider.  
- **Update your master password** – If you have not changed it recently, choose a strong, unique master password that you do not reuse elsewhere.  
- **Enable two‑factor authentication (2FA)** – Turn on 2FA for your 1Password account if it is not already active.  
- **Review saved credentials** – Verify that the passwords stored in the vault are still strong; replace any that are weak or reused.  
- **Watch for phishing attempts** – Be cautious of unexpected emails or messages that reference 1Password, especially those asking for login details or verification codes.  
- **Keep software current** – Ensure the 1Password app on all devices is updated to the latest version, and apply any security patches promptly.
