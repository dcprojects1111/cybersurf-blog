The tech world is buzzing with new phones, smarter car navigation and a few handy fixes for everyday gadgets. Here’s what you need to know, explained in plain language.

## Xiaomi 18 spotted in live photo – first look at the upcoming flagship

A tipster on X (a social platform) posted what appears to be the first live image of the Xiaomi 18, the company’s next flagship phone. The white device shows a metal frame and a “squircle” (rounded‑square) camera module that houses three lenses. Unlike the higher‑end Xiaomi 18 Pro models, this version does **not** have a secondary display on the back.

**What this means for you**  
- If you’re thinking about upgrading your phone, the Xiaomi 18 could be an option later this year, but Australian pricing and release dates haven’t been announced yet.  
- Keep an eye on local retailers such as JB Hi‑Fi, Harvey Norman or your mobile carrier for official information.  
- The lack of a rear secondary screen may make the phone a bit slimmer and possibly cheaper than the Pro models, but you’ll need to wait for the full specs to be confirmed.

## New EV routing built into CarPlay and Android Auto

CarPlay (Apple’s in‑car interface) and Android Auto (Google’s equivalent) now include a feature that maps out electric‑vehicle charging stops automatically. When you start a trip, the system looks for compatible chargers along the route and can suggest the best places to stop, helping you avoid range anxiety on long drives.

**What this means for you**  
- If you own an electric car, you can set up the routing directly from your phone’s navigation app, no extra hardware required.  
- Before you rely on it, make sure your car’s infotainment system is updated to the latest software version.  
- The feature works with most major charging networks, but it’s worth checking that the chargers shown are compatible with your vehicle’s plug type.

## Microsoft’s Copilot now runs Word, Excel and PowerPoint inside it

At its Ignite keynote, Microsoft described Copilot as “the new OS for work.” In practical terms, the familiar Office apps—Word, Excel and PowerPoint—are now embedded inside the Copilot environment, meaning they launch from the same place and can interact with Copilot’s AI helpers.

**What this means for you**  
- If you use Microsoft Office at home or in a small business, you’ll soon see the apps open inside a single, AI‑enhanced workspace.  
- The change should be seamless; you’ll still be able to create documents, spreadsheets and presentations as before, but with the added option of asking Copilot for suggestions or quick edits.  
- No new hardware is required, but you’ll need a Microsoft 365 subscription to access the feature when it rolls out.

## Google Gemini lets you view and edit what the AI remembers about you

Google has added a new privacy tool for its Gemini AI model that shows you a summary of the information the system has stored about you. You can now edit or delete specific items, giving you more control over how the AI tailors its responses.

**What this means for you**  
- If you use Gemini for writing help, research or chat, you can now clean out old or inaccurate data that might be influencing its answers.  
- This feature is available now, but you’ll need a Google account linked to Gemini to access it.  
- **Tip:** Review the memory summary regularly and delete anything you don’t want the AI to use, especially personal details you’ve shared in the past.

## Simple fixes when your PC won’t read an external hard drive

External hard drives are handy for backing up photos, videos and documents, but they sometimes refuse to show up on a PC or Mac. The latest guide walks you through easy hardware checks—like trying a different USB port or cable—and software steps such as restarting the computer, updating drivers or using the built‑in disk utility to repair the drive.

**What this means for you**  
- Most read‑errors can be solved without a technician; try the listed steps before assuming the drive is dead.  
- If the drive still won’t appear, consider copying any important files to another storage device as soon as possible.  
- **Tip:** Regularly back up your data to more than one location (e.g., a cloud service plus an external drive) to avoid loss if a drive fails.

- The Xiaomi 18 is on the horizon; watch for Australian release info.  
- EV routing in CarPlay/Android Auto makes electric‑car road trips easier.  
- Microsoft’s Copilot now houses Office apps, bringing AI assistance into everyday work.  
- Google Gemini’s memory editor gives you more privacy control.  
- A few simple steps can often revive an unresponsive external hard drive.
