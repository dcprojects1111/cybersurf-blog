The tech world is buzzing with a mix of new gadgets and software updates that could soon find their way onto Australian shelves or into your daily routine. From a possible new fold‑over phone to an AI‑powered companion device, here’s what’s worth noting this week.

## Motorola’s foldable phone could be the next big thing

Leaked images have surfaced that appear to show Motorola’s answer to the iPhone Duo’s foldable design. The pictures suggest a device that folds inward, similar to other “dual‑screen” phones, and hint at a thin hinge and a large outer display.

**What this means for you**  
- If you’ve been waiting for a foldable phone that isn’t a Samsung, Motorola might soon give you an alternative.  
- The phone isn’t officially announced yet, so there’s no confirmed Australian release date or price. Keep an eye on local retailers such as JB Hi‑Fi, Harvey Norman or the Motorola website for updates.  

## Meta’s Muse Charm AI gadget

Meta is reportedly preparing a standalone AI device called the Muse Charm, priced at about US $300 and bundled with a free Verizon 5G data plan. The gadget is designed to run Meta’s AI models locally, acting as a voice‑assistant that can answer questions, control smart home devices and more without needing a phone.

**What this means for you**  
- The Muse Charm could become a handy hands‑free helper around the house, especially if you already use Meta services.  
- The price shown is a US figure; Australian pricing and availability have not been confirmed. If the device reaches Australia, expect it to appear through major electronics stores or online marketplaces.  

## Whoop becomes the official health wearable for Formula 1

Whoop, the fitness tracker known for its continuous heart‑rate monitoring, has secured a four‑year partnership as the Official Health & Fitness Wearable Partner of Formula 1. Starting in 2027, live physiological data from drivers will be shown on TV broadcasts, and drivers will be allowed to wear Whoop devices during races.

**What this means for you**  
- The partnership highlights the credibility of Whoop’s sensor technology, which may reassure existing or prospective users about its accuracy.  
- Whoop devices are already sold in Australia, so you can continue to buy them from local retailers or directly from Whoop’s website. The F1 tie‑in doesn’t change pricing or features for everyday users.  

## OpenAI tests “ChatGPT dot” with Plus subscribers

OpenAI has begun a small‑scale test of a new feature called “ChatGPT dot” with a handful of ChatGPT Plus users. The feature follows earlier roll‑outs for ChatGPT Pro, Business Premium and Enterprise customers, and aims to add extra functionality for paying subscribers.

**What this means for you**  
- If you already pay for ChatGPT Plus, you might see a new button or option appear in the app soon. It’s a trial, so the exact benefits aren’t fully detailed yet.  
- The service is cloud‑based, so it works on any device with an internet connection. No hardware changes are needed, and Australian pricing for Plus remains the same as listed on OpenAI’s website.  

## macOS 27.3 public beta is now available

Apple has released the second public beta of macOS 27.3, following an earlier developer preview. The beta lets Mac owners test upcoming features ahead of the official launch scheduled for next month. Details of the new features are listed on the Apple developer site.

**What this means for you**  
- If you run a Mac and like trying new software early, you can enroll in the public beta through Apple’s website. Be aware that beta software can be less stable than the final release.  
- The update is free for existing Mac users and works on any Mac that supports macOS 27.2, so there’s no extra cost or hardware upgrade required.  

## Quick takeaway

- Motorola’s foldable phone is still a rumor, but it could give Australian consumers another choice in the emerging foldable market.  
- Meta’s Muse Charm is an AI assistant priced in US dollars; watch for local pricing before deciding.  
- Whoop’s F1 partnership reinforces its fitness‑tracker reputation, with no immediate changes for Australian users.  
- OpenAI’s ChatGPT dot is a trial feature for Plus subscribers—no extra hardware needed.  
- macOS 27.3 public beta is free to test on compatible Macs, though it may be a bit rough around the edges.
