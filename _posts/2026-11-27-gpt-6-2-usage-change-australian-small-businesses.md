## OpenAI’s GPT‑6.2 Usage Change – What It Means for Your Small Business  

OpenAI has announced a correction to a billing bug that previously gave users an unintended discount on the newer GPT‑6.2 Astra and Sol models. The fix means that the same amount of work will now consume **40 % more of your usage allowance on Astra** and **25 % more on Sol**. While OpenAI says it will not charge for the extra consumption and is adding a one‑time usage reset for eligible plans, the change still matters for anyone relying on AI tools for everyday business tasks.

### Why This Update Matters to Small Business Owners  

- **Budget Impact** – Many sole traders and home‑based businesses use ChatGPT under a subscription (Go, Plus, Pro or Business). Faster consumption of tokens can mean you hit your monthly limit sooner, potentially prompting an upgrade or additional costs.  
- **Productivity Planning** – If you schedule AI‑generated content, customer replies, or inventory forecasts, the reduced allowance could interrupt workflows unless you monitor usage closely.  
- **Choice of Model** – OpenAI notes that older models (e.g., GPT‑6 Astra, GPT‑6.1 Sol) retain their previous efficiency calculations. Switching back to a less‑recent model may stretch your allowance further, though performance may differ.  

In short, the change is not a security incident, but it does affect **small business cyber security Australia** budgets and the way you manage AI‑driven processes. Understanding how your AI tools consume resources helps you avoid unexpected expenses and keep your operations running smoothly.

### Quick Checklist – Managing AI Usage in Your Business  

- **Review Your Plan**  
  - Log into ChatGPT, go to **Settings → Usage**, and note your current token consumption.  
  - If you’re on a plan that received the free reset, use it within the month to regain lost allowance.  

- **Set Usage Alerts**  
  - Many AI platforms let you set notifications when you reach a certain percentage of your limit.  
  - Treat these alerts like the usage caps you’d set on internet data or phone plans.  

- **Prioritise Critical Tasks**  
  - Reserve the newer GPT‑6.2 models for high‑value activities (e.g., drafting contracts, creating marketing copy).  
  - Use older, more token‑efficient models for routine tasks such as simple email replies.  

- **Document AI Interactions**  
  - Keep a brief log of what you asked the AI to do and the model used. This helps you spot patterns where token usage spikes.  

- **Consider Alternative Tools**  
  - If token limits become a recurring bottleneck, explore local Australian AI services or open‑source options that may have different pricing structures.  

### General Cyber‑Security Advice for AI Users  

Even though this announcement is about usage limits, it’s a good reminder to keep your broader **small business cyber security** posture strong:

- **Protect Access Credentials** – Store passwords in a reputable manager (e.g., NordPass) and avoid sharing them in AI prompts.  
- **Watch for Phishing** – AI‑generated text can be used in phishing or business‑email‑compromise scams. Verify unexpected requests through a known channel.  
- **Report Suspicious Activity** – If you notice unusual login attempts on your OpenAI account, report them to the Australian Cyber Security Centre (ACSC) via [cyber.gov.au](https://www.cyber.gov.au) or to ReportCyber.  

### Australian Resources You Can Trust

- **Scamwatch** – Up‑to‑date information on the latest scams targeting Australian businesses.  
- **OAIC (Office of the Australian Information Commissioner)** – Advice on handling personal data and complying with privacy laws after a data breach.  

### Bottom Line  

OpenAI’s correction to GPT‑6.2 usage calculations won’t directly expose your business to a cyber threat, but it does affect **SME data breach Australia** considerations indirectly. Faster token consumption can lead to unplanned spending, which may force you to cut corners elsewhere—potentially weakening your overall security posture. By monitoring usage, leveraging the free reset, and keeping your broader cyber‑security practices up to date, you can continue to benefit from AI without surprising costs.
