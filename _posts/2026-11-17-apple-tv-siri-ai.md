Apple’s latest software tweaks and a few rumours are shaping what you might see on your TV screen, phone and even in your Instagram inbox. Here’s a plain‑English rundown of the most relevant consumer tech news for everyday Australians.

## Siri AI lands on more Apple TVs

Apple has pushed the next version of its tvOS 27.3 beta to a wider group of testers, adding the new Siri AI voice assistant to compatible Apple TV models. Siri AI is an upgraded version of the familiar voice helper, now powered by artificial‑intelligence techniques that let it understand more natural conversation.

**What this means for you**  
- If you already own an Apple TV, the feature is still in a testing phase, so you won’t see it unless you join the beta programme.  
- For most Australians, there’s no immediate change, but the rollout hints that future Apple TV updates may let you ask the TV to do things like find shows, control smart‑home devices or answer questions without needing a separate remote.  
- Keep an eye on Apple’s official announcements and check the Apple Store or your provider for the next public software release.

## Samsung may bring variable‑aperture cameras back on its flagship phones

A new tipster on X (formerly Twitter) suggests Samsung could re‑introduce “variable aperture” on the upcoming Galaxy S28 Pro and S28 Ultra models. Variable aperture means the camera lens can open wider or narrower (changing the f‑number) depending on lighting, similar to a DSLR camera, which can improve low‑light performance and depth‑of‑field control.

**What this means for you**  
- It’s still a rumor, so no Samsung phone on Australian shelves has confirmed this feature yet.  
- If you’re planning to upgrade your phone later this year, you may want to wait for the official launch details before deciding, especially if camera flexibility is important to you.  
- When the phones become available, check local retailers such as JB Hi‑Fi, Harvey Norman or your mobile carrier for the exact specifications and pricing.

## Meta testing “Direct” – a separate Instagram messaging app

Meta (the company behind Facebook and Instagram) is trialling a stand‑alone app called Direct that handles Instagram direct messages. The test is currently limited to users in Turkey and Thailand, and the app is not yet released elsewhere.

**What this means for you**  
- For most Australians, Instagram messages will still be sent and read inside the main Instagram app.  
- If Meta eventually rolls out Direct in Australia, it could mean a lighter, faster way to chat without loading the full Instagram feed.  
- Until then, there’s no need to change anything on your phone; just keep the regular Instagram app updated for the latest features and security patches.

## Buying a used phone? Check the IMEI first

When purchasing a second‑hand smartphone, the IMEI – a unique 15‑digit identifier printed inside the device – can reveal whether the phone has been reported lost, stolen or blocked by carriers. Engadget advises checking this number before you hand over cash.

**What this means for you**  
- Locate the IMEI on the phone (usually in Settings → About, or printed on the back of the device) and type it into an online IMEI checker or ask your carrier to verify its status.  
- A clean IMEI means the phone is less likely to have hidden problems, giving you peace of mind before you buy.  
- If the IMEI shows a negative history, walk away – it could end up blocked from making calls or connecting to data networks.

**Quick takeaway**

- Apple TV’s Siri AI is still in beta, so expect it to appear in a future public update.  
- Samsung’s variable‑aperture camera is only a rumor; wait for official specs before upgrading.  
- Meta’s Direct messaging app is in a limited test and not yet relevant for Australian users.  
- Always verify a used phone’s IMEI to avoid hidden issues.
